Your child

is a Sensitive Observer

They don't miss a thing. They experience the world in high definition—the sounds are louder, the lights brighter, and the feelings deeper.

The "High-Def" World

Your child likely has Sensory Processing Sensitivity. Their brain filters less information than others, leading to quicker overwhelm.

  • They cover their ears at loud noises (hand dryers, sirens).
  • They are extremely picky about socks, tags, and food textures.
  • They prefer to watch from the sidelines before joining a group.
  • They absorb your stress like a sponge, even when you hide it.
🧽

The Sponge: They soak up the emotional energy of every room they enter.

🔊

The Sensor: Background noise that you ignore sounds like a megaphone to them.

👀

The Watcher: They are cautious, needing to map out a situation before feeling safe.

They aren't "too shy" or "too dramatic."

They are simply receiving more data than their nervous system can process. They need tools to filter the noise.

Try This Tonight: "The Volume Control"

When they are overwhelmed, words often make it worse. Use a signal instead.

1The Concept: Create a hand signal (like a "time out" T or touching the nose) that means "It's too loud/much."

2The Promise: If they use it, you promise to let them step away immediately, no questions asked.

Knowing they have an "escape hatch" often reduces their anxiety enough that they don't need to use it.
