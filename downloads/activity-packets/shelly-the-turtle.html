What learned today

🎯 Skills Practiced

1. Emotional Intensity Awareness (Wave Check)

Rating feelings on a 1-5 scale builds metacognition - the ability to observe one's own emotional state. This is foundational for regulation because intensity determines which strategies are appropriate.

2. Tactile-Guided Breathing (Starfish Breathing)

Finger tracing provides a physical anchor that keeps focus on the breath, making it easier for young children than breath-only exercises. The up/down motion naturally slows breathing rate.

3. Physical Energy Discharge (Shake It Out)

Big emotions create physical activation (increased heart rate, muscle tension). Shaking or movement helps discharge this energy safely rather than suppressing it. This is especially helpful for kids who need to move.

4. Affect Labeling (Name Your Wave)

Research shows that simply naming an emotion reduces amygdala activation. When children say "I'm feeling a mad wave," it creates cognitive distance from the feeling and engages the prefrontal cortex.

💬 Conversation Starters
  • "What size wave are you feeling right now?"
  • "Want to do Starfish Breathing together?"
  • "Let's shake like fish before dinner!"
  • "What would you name that wave you're feeling?"
🏠 Practice Ideas
  • Morning check-in: "What size wave are you starting the day with?"
  • Car rides: Starfish Breathing is perfect for transitions
  • After school: "Let's shake like fish to get the wiggles out!"
  • Bedtime: Practice naming waves from the day
⚠️ When Waves Are Crashing

When your child is already flooded (level 5 wave), tools are harder to access. Try:

  • Co-regulate first - be their calm anchor
  • Offer movement: "Want to shake like fish with me?"
  • Wait, then reconnect: "That was a big wave. You rode it."
  • Avoid teaching in the moment - practice when calm
