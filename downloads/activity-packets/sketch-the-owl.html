What learned today

🎯 Skills Practiced

1. Lowering the Starting Bar (Sloppy Copy)

Perfectionism often prevents starting. By explicitly giving permission for messy first attempts, we remove the paralysis of "it has to be good." The phrase "first tries are supposed to be messy" normalizes the drafting process that all creators use.

2. Cognitive Reframing (Good Enough Goggles)

The "goggles" create a mental shift from impossible standards to realistic ones. When children ask "Is this good enough?" instead of "Is this perfect?", they can actually evaluate their work fairly. This is a CBT-style reframing tool.

3. Growth Mindset (The Magic YET)

Adding "yet" to fixed statements transforms them into growth statements. "I can't draw" becomes "I can't draw yet" - implying learning is possible. Research by Carol Dweck shows this simple word shift changes how children approach challenges.

4. Completion Over Perfection (Finish Line Fun)

Perfectionists often have many unfinished projects. Celebrating completion - regardless of quality - builds the habit of finishing. This creates dopamine reward for DONE rather than only for PERFECT, which is often unattainable.

💬 Conversation Starters
  • "Want to make a sloppy copy first?"
  • "Let's put on our Good Enough Goggles - is this good enough?"
  • "Can you add YET to that thought?"
  • "Let's celebrate that you FINISHED!"
🏠 Practice Ideas
  • Model imperfection: Let them see YOUR messy first drafts
  • Sloppy copy time: Set a timer for a fast, messy first attempt
  • YET jar: Catch "I can't" statements and add YET together
  • Done celebrations: High-five finishing, not just quality
  • Display imperfect work: Put up art that's "good enough"
⚠️ When Perfectionism Takes Over

Deep thinking and high standards are strengths! But when they cause suffering:

  • Don't dismiss: "It looks fine!" invalidates their experience
  • Name it: "Your brain is being a perfectionist right now"
  • Time limits: "You have 10 more minutes, then it's done"
  • External deadline: "The gallery closes at 5!" (playfully)
  • Separate worth from work: "I love YOU, not just what you make"
