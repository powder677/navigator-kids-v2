🎯 Skills Practiced

1. Interoceptive Awareness (Volume Dial)

Sensory-sensitive children often don't recognize they're becoming overwhelmed until they're already flooded. The "Volume Dial" teaches them to notice their internal state BEFORE crisis. This early awareness creates time to use tools.

2. Environmental Modification (Cozy Cocoon)

Teaching children they can CREATE calmer environments gives them agency. A "Cozy Cocoon" might be noise-canceling headphones, a dim corner, or a weighted blanket. Identifying what helps THEM is key - sensory needs are individual.

3. Sensory Grounding (Gentle Grounding)

When overwhelmed, the nervous system is in "threat" mode. Gentle sensory input (soft textures, quiet sounds) signals SAFETY to the brain. This is a gentler version of 5-4-3-2-1 grounding, adapted for sensory-sensitive kids.

4. Self-Advocacy (Ask for What I Need)

Many sensory-sensitive children suffer silently or melt down without warning. Learning to verbalize needs ("This is too loud for me") prevents escalation. Practice the WORDS when calm so they're accessible when overwhelmed.

💬 Conversation Starters
  • "What's your volume dial at right now?"
  • "Do you need your Cozy Cocoon?"
  • "Let's do some gentle grounding together."
  • "What words could you use to ask for what you need?"
🏠 Practice Ideas
  • Create a physical Cozy Cocoon: A corner with soft items, dim light, headphones
  • Volume check-ins: Ask about their dial at transitions
  • Sensory kit: A bag with fidgets, sunglasses, ear plugs for outings
  • Practice phrases: Role-play asking for breaks/quiet/space
  • Honor their needs: When they ask, respond - this builds trust
⚠️ Sensitivity is a Superpower

Sensory sensitivity often comes with gifts: noticing beauty others miss, deep empathy, attention to detail. Reframe it as a superpower that needs management, not a flaw to fix.

  • Validate: "Your ears ARE more sensitive - that's real"
  • Don't force: Gradual exposure works better than flooding
  • Plan ahead: Know triggers and have tools ready
  • Model it: "I need a quiet break too"
