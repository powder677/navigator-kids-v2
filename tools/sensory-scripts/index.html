🌊 Sensory Overload Script

Use when your child is overwhelmed by their environment—too much noise, light, activity, or stimulation.

Recognizing the Signs

  • Covering ears or eyes
  • Trying to escape or hide
  • Becoming aggressive or "shutting down"
  • Saying "it's too much" or "I can't"

Calming Script (Say in a low, slow voice)

Step 1: Acknowledge

"I can see this is too much right now. That's okay. I'm here."

Step 2: Reduce Input

"Let's find somewhere quieter. Can you come with me, or do you need me to wait?"

Step 3: Ground

"Let's take 3 slow breaths together. In... 2... 3... Out... 2... 3..."

Step 4: Offer Tools

"Would headphones help? Or would you like to squeeze my hand really tight?"

What NOT to Say

  • ❌ "It's not that loud/bright/etc."
  • ❌ "Everyone else is fine."
  • ❌ "You need to calm down."
  • ❌ "Stop being dramatic."

👕 Clothing Battles Script

Use when tags, seams, or textures make getting dressed a daily battle.

Prevention Strategies

  • Cut ALL tags (even the tiny ones)
  • Wash new clothes multiple times before wearing
  • Buy duplicates of clothes that work
  • Let them choose from 2-3 pre-approved options
  • Inside-out for exposed seams

In-the-Moment Script

Validate

"I believe you that it feels wrong. Bodies are different and yours is telling you something."

Problem-Solve Together

"Let's figure this out. Is it the waist? The sleeves? Can you show me where it bothers you?"

Offer Alternatives

"Would you like to try the soft pants instead? Or we can adjust this one?"

For School/Events

"I know this isn't your favorite, but we need to wear [item] for [event]. Can we do a test run for 10 minutes? If it's still unbearable, we'll problem-solve."

🔊 Noise Sensitivity Script

Use when sounds that others tolerate cause distress or physical discomfort.

Proactive Tools

  • Noise-canceling headphones
  • Earplugs (loop earplugs for kids)
  • Warning before loud events
  • Escape plan for overwhelming situations

When They're Distressed

Acknowledge Reality

"That sound is really bothering you. Some people's ears are more sensitive—yours are picking up things others don't even notice."

Offer Control

"Would headphones help? Or would you like to move somewhere quieter?"

For Unavoidable Noise

"This sound will last until [time/event]. Let's count down together / use headphones / focus on this other thing."

🍽️ Food Texture Script

Use when picky eating is driven by sensory needs, not preference.

Understanding Sensory-Based Eating

  • This is NOT manipulation or willfulness
  • Certain textures can trigger gag reflexes
  • Mixed textures are often harder than single textures
  • Temperature and smell matter too

Mealtime Script

Pressure-Free Exposure

"This food is here if you want to try it. No pressure. You can touch it, smell it, or just look at it."

When They Refuse

"That's okay. Your body is telling you something. There's [safe food] if you're hungry."

Celebrating Trying

"You licked it! That's so brave. You don't have to eat it—just learning about it is great."

✋ Touch Aversion Script

Use when your child pulls away from touch or is bothered by physical contact.

Teaching Others

"[Child]'s body works differently. They like hugs sometimes but not always. Let's ask first: '[Child], would you like a hug, high-five, or wave?'"

When They Pull Away

"I noticed that touch didn't feel good right now. Thanks for listening to your body. Would you like space, or a different kind of connection?"

Offering Alternatives

"Instead of a hug, would you like a: firm squeeze on your shoulders? Fist bump? Just sitting close? Or nothing right now?"

💨 Sensory Seeking Script

Use when your child needs MORE input—crashing, spinning, chewing, or constant movement.

Healthy Seeking Activities

  • Heavy work: carrying groceries, pushing laundry basket
  • Crash pad or pile of pillows
  • Spinning in office chair (with supervision)
  • Chew toys or crunchy snacks
  • Tight hugs or weighted blanket
  • Jumping, climbing, swinging

Redirect to Safe Seeking

"I can see your body needs to MOVE! Let's find a safe way. Would you like to jump on the trampoline, do wall push-ups, or carry this heavy basket for me?"

Proactive Sensory Diet

"Before we go to [low-input activity], let's fill your body's tank. 10 jumps, 5 spins, then squeezes!"
