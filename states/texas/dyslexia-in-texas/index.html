Dyslexia in Texas: Why a 504 Plan Isn't Enough

"Your child is smart. He just needs extra time on tests. We'll put him on a Section 504 plan. He doesn't need Special Education."

If you are a parent of a dyslexic child in Texas, you have likely heard this script word-for-word. For decades, Texas schools diverted students with dyslexia into Section 504 plans rather than Individualized Education Programs (IEPs).

But here is the problem: A 504 plan is designed for Access. An IEP is designed for Instruction. If your child cannot read, "access" (audiobooks) isn't enough. They need instruction. Thanks to House Bill 3928, the "Texas Loophole" has officially closed.

The New Rule: Dyslexia IS Special Education

Under HB 3928, if your child needs "Standard Protocol Dyslexia Instruction" (Take Flight, MTA, etc.), that is now legally defined as Specially Designed Instruction (SDI). By definition, if your child needs SDI, they qualify for an IEP.

504 vs. IEP: The instruction Gap

  • The Goal: 504 ensures Equal Access. IEP ensures Progress.
  • The Help: 504 provides Accommodations (extra time). IEP provides Intervention (Certified Therapist).
  • Accountability: 504 has no required goals. IEP requires Measurable Goals and progress reports.

The "Accommodations Trap"

Accommodations are band-aids. Giving a child an audiobook allows them to pass today, but it doesn't teach them to read tomorrow. Use your IEP Battle Plan to demand instruction over mere access.
