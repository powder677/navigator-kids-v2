Texas ARD Meeting Script: Exact Words to Use When Schools Say "No"

You walk into the conference room. There is one of you, and six of them. The Principal, the Counselor, the Diagnostician, the General Ed teacher, the Special Ed teacher, and someone from "District" you’ve never met.

They sit on one side of the table. You sit on the other.

This is the ARD (Admission, Review, and Dismissal) meeting. In Texas, this is where the decisions are made. And too often, it is where parents get steamrolled.

They have a stack of data. They have acronyms you don't understand. And they have a favorite word: "No."

But you have something stronger: The Law.

You do not need to be a lawyer to win an ARD meeting. You just need the right scripts. Here are the exact phrases to use when the school tries to shut you down, grounded in Texas Education Code and IDEA.

Scenario 1: The "Policy" Block

This is the most common shut-down tactic. The school claims their hands are tied by a "district rule".

The School Says: "We don't offer Orton-Gillingham therapy in this district. We only use the standard reading curriculum."

You Say:

"I understand that is the general policy. However, the law requires the IEP to be individualized based on my child's unique needs, not based on administrative convenience or available staff. Can you show me the data that proves your standard curriculum will meet his specific deficit in phonological processing? If not, we need to discuss 'Specially Designed Instruction' that does meet that need."

Why it works: In Endrew F. v. Douglas County, the Supreme Court ruled that "de minimis" (bare minimum) progress is not enough. An IEP must be "appropriately ambitious".

Scenario 2: The "Good Grades" Defense

This is the classic trap for Twice-Exceptional (2e) students (gifted kids with disabilities).

The School Says: "He has all A's and B's. He doesn't need an IEP; he's doing fine."

You Say:

"Grades are not the sole determinant of eligibility. Under 34 CFR §300.101(c), a child is entitled to FAPE even if they are advancing from grade to grade. We need to look at the discrepancy between his intelligence and his performance, and the emotional toll it takes for him to maintain those grades. I am requesting an evaluation of his Executive Functioning and processing speed, not just his report card."

Scenario 3: The "We Can't Afford That" (Implied)

They will rarely say "we have no money," but they will say "we don't have the staff for that".

The School Says: "We don't have a dedicated aide available for that time block."

You Say:

"Please correct me if I am wrong, but my understanding of IDEA is that a lack of resources cannot be used as a justification for denying FAPE. If the ARD committee agrees he needs this support to access the curriculum, the district is obligated to provide it, regardless of current staffing levels."

The Ultimate Weapon: "Prior Written Notice"

If they still say "No" after you use these scripts, do not get angry. Get procedural.

The Script

"I hear that you are refusing my request for [Service]. I am officially requesting Prior Written Notice (PWN) for that refusal. I want the PWN to state exactly why you are refusing, what data you used to make that decision, and what other options were considered."

Why this is magic: Administrators hate writing PWNs. It forces them to create a legal document admitting they denied a service. Often, they will say, "Well, let's see if we can work something out..." just to avoid writing the letter.

The Texas Nuclear Option: The 10-Day Recess

In Texas (and only Texas), parents have a superpower called the "10-Day Recess" (19 TAC §89.1050(g)). If the meeting is going badly and you disagree with the IEP, do not sign "Agree."

The Script to End the Meeting:

"I do not agree with this IEP as written. Under Texas Administrative Code, I am invoking my right to a 10-day recess. I would like to stop the meeting, gather more data, and reconvene in 10 school days to try to reach a consensus."

This stops the clock. It forces the district to go back to the drawing board. It gives you time to call an advocate.
