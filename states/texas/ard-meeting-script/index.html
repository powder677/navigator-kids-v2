Texas ARD Meeting Script: Exact Words to Use When Schools Say "No"

You walk into the conference room. There is one of you, and six of them. The Principal, the Counselor, the Diagnostician, the General Ed teacher, the Special Ed teacher, and someone from "District" you’ve never met.

They sit on one side of the table. You sit on the other.

This is the ARD (Admission, Review, and Dismissal) meeting. In Texas, this is where the decisions are made. And too often, it is where parents get steamrolled.

Scenario 1: The "Policy" Block

This is the most common shut-down tactic. The school claims their hands are tied by a "district rule".

The School Says: "We don't offer Orton-Gillingham therapy in this district. We only use the standard reading curriculum."

You Say:

"I understand that is general policy. However, the law requires the IEP to be individualized based on my child's unique needs, not based on administrative convenience. Can you show me the data that proves your standard curriculum will meet his specific deficit? If not, we need to discuss Specially Designed Instruction."

Scenario 2: The "Good Grades" Defense

Classic trap for Twice-Exceptional (2e) students.

The School Says: "He has all A's and B's. He doesn't need an IEP."

You Say:

"Grades are not the sole determinant of eligibility. Under 34 CFR §300.101(c), a child is entitled to FAPE even if advancing grade to grade. We need to look at the discrepancy between his intelligence and his performance."

The Texas Nuclear Option: The 10-Day Recess

In Texas (and only Texas), parents have a superpower called the "10-Day Recess" (19 TAC §89.1050(g)).

Script to End the Meeting:

"I do not agree with this IEP as written. Under Texas Administrative Code, I am invoking my right to a 10-day recess. I would like to stop the meeting, gather more data, and reconvene in 10 school days."
