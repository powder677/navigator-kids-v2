Jurisdiction: Florida

Florida: Where "Gifted" is Special Education

In the Sunshine State, we use the term ESE (Exceptional Student Education). Unlike most states, Florida law classifies "Gifted" under the ESE umbrella, granting gifted students many of the same procedural safeguards as students with disabilities.

⏱️ The "30-60" Rule: Florida Timelines

Florida strict statutes govern how long a school can make you wait (Rule 6A-6.0331).

Action Florida Deadline Parent Strategy
You Request an Evaluation 30 Calendar Days to respond. The school must either get your written consent to evaluate OR give you a written notice explaining why they refuse.
The Evaluation Process 60 Calendar Days to complete. The clock starts the day the school receives your signed consent form. (Note: Breaks of 5+ days pause the clock).
The Eligibility Meeting Reasonable Time (Usually 30 days). Once the report is done, the team meets to determine eligibility for ESE services.

Your Florida-Specific Powers

🧠 The "Gifted is ESE" Advantage

The Law: In Florida, Gifted is an ESE program. This means 2e kids are technically "Double ESE."

The Strategy: If the school tries to give you an EP (Educational Plan) for giftedness and a 504 for the disability, push back. You can argue for a comprehensive IEP that covers both the advanced cognitive needs and the disability deficits in one legal document.

💸 The "Nuclear Option": FES-UA

The Program: The Family Empowerment Scholarship for Students with Unique Abilities (formerly McKay/Gardiner).

The Power: If the public school cannot meet your child's needs, Florida allows you to take the state funding (often $10k+) and use it for private school, tutoring, or therapy. The mere existence of this option often makes districts work harder to keep you.

📊 The Matrix of Services

The Form: Every IEP student in FL gets a "Matrix Score" (251, 252, etc.) which determines how much money the school gets for your child.

The Strategy: Ask to see the Matrix. If they say they don't have the budget for an aide, check the Matrix. If your child is scored as a "254" or "255" (high needs), the school is receiving extra funds specifically for that support.

2e in the Sunshine State

Florida's "Plan B" for Gifted eligibility is a massive tool for 2e families.

Plan B: The Gifted Loophole

Most kids qualify for Gifted with an IQ of 130 (Plan A). But Florida offers "Plan B" for underrepresented groups or students whose test scores are masked by a disability (like 2e kids).

Action Item: If your child has an IQ of 125 but has ADHD/Autism that depressed their score, ask for eligibility under Plan B (Rule 6A-6.03019). This allows a matrix of criteria (creativity, teacher checklists) to replace the strict IQ cutoff.
