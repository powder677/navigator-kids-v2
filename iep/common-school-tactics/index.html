They Say "Policy." You Say "Individualized."

Schools operate on budgets and averages. Your child is an individual. Learn how to spot when a "No" is based on bureaucracy rather than the law.

The Gatekeeping Dictionary

If you hear these phrases, your "Spidey Sense" should tingle. These are often used to delay or deny services for your child.

🚩

"We don't do that here."

"We don't offer 1:1 aides in this building," or "We don't use that specific reading program."

The Counter:

"The 'I' in IEP stands for Individualized. If the team determines my child needs this service to receive FAPE, the district is required to provide it, regardless of current building policy."

🚩

"Let's wait and see."

"He's transitioning. Let's give it another grading period to see if he settles in."

The Counter:

"Wait and Fail is not an intervention. We already have data showing my child is struggling. Delaying support harms their self-esteem. I am requesting an evaluation now."

🚩

"He has good grades."

"He can't have a disability; he's getting A's and B's." (The classic anti-2e argument).

The Counter:

"Grades are not the only metric of educational performance. IDEA requires we look at functional, social, and emotional performance. His grades are high, but his anxiety/executive function is preventing him from accessing school safely."

⚠️ The "Behavior is a Choice" Trap

For 2e kids, schools often label meltdowns or work refusal as "defiance."

The Trap: "He chooses not to do the work. He's smart enough to do it, he's just being lazy."

The Reality: Behavior is communication. "Laziness" is often overwhelmed executive function or fear of failure.

Your Script: "If he could, he would. Since he isn't, something is blocking him. We need a Functional Behavior Assessment (FBA) to find out what the trigger is, rather than punishing the result."

The Ultimate Counter-Move: "Put it in the PWN"

When the school says "No" to a request (like an evaluation, an aide, or a service), do not just accept it. Make them own it legally.

"I understand the team is refusing my request for [Service] for my child. Please ensure that this refusal, and the specific data used to justify it, is documented in the Prior Written Notice (PWN)."

Why this works: Administrators hate writing refusals in the PWN because that document is Exhibit A in a due process hearing. Often, simply asking for the PWN makes them reconsider the refusal.

When They Won't Budge: The Escalation Ladder

  1. The Case Manager: Start here. "Can we check the data?"
  2. The Principal: "I'm concerned the IEP isn't being implemented for my child."
  3. Director of Special Education: "The school team is stating they cannot provide FAPE due to staffing. I need your help to resolve this resource issue."
  4. State Complaint: Filing a formal complaint with your State Department of Education.
