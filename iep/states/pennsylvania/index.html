Jurisdiction: Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania: The Land of the NOREP

In the Keystone State, Special Education is governed by Chapter 14 and Gifted Education by Chapter 16. Understanding how these two laws interact is the key to securing a program that supports your child's deficits and feeds their strengths.

⏱️ The "60-Day" Summer Pause

Pennsylvania uses calendar days, but with a major catch for the summer break.

Action PA Deadline Parent Strategy
Permission to Evaluate (PTE) 60 Calendar Days. The school must complete the evaluation report (ER) within 60 days of receiving your signed PTE form.
The "Summer Stop" Clock Pauses in Summer. Critical: The 60-day clock stops the day school ends and restarts the day school begins. If you sign permission in May, do not expect a report until October.
The IEP Meeting 30 Calendar Days. Once the Evaluation Report (ER) is issued, the IEP team must meet within 30 calendar days.

Your PA-Specific Powers

📝 The NOREP (The "Big Deal")

The Document: The Notice of Recommended Educational Placement. This is the document where you officially agree or disagree.

The Trap: For changes to an existing IEP, if you receive a NOREP and do not respond within 10 days, the district may be able to implement the changes automatically.

The Strategy: Never ignore a NOREP. Check "I disapprove," write a brief reason, and return it immediately to freeze the current program (Stay Put).

🎤 Recording: The "Wiretap" Warning

The Law: PA is a "Two-Party Consent" state under the Wiretap Act. Secretly recording a meeting is a felony.

The Strategy: You can record, but you must ask for permission. Many districts have policies allowing it, but if they say no, take detailed minutes and email them immediately after.

📅 The "10-Day Waiver"

The Event: At the evaluation meeting, they will ask you to sign a "Waiver of the 10-Day Wait."

The Meaning: PA law gives you 10 days to read the Evaluation Report before the IEP meeting occurs. If you sign the waiver, the IEP meeting happens immediately.

Advice: Do not sign the waiver unless the report is perfect. Take your 10 days to read the report regarding your child at home.

2e in Pennsylvania: The "Dual" System

PA is unique because it has a specific law for Gifted Students (Chapter 16) separate from Special Ed (Chapter 14).

The GIEP vs. IEP Battle

If your child is 2e, they technically qualify for both.

  • The GIEP: Gifted Individualized Education Plan (Focus on strength).
  • The IEP: Individualized Education Program (Focus on disability).

The Strategy: Legally, the IEP "trumps" the GIEP. Usually, the team will roll the gifted goals into the IEP.
Watch Out: Ensure the Gifted goals don't disappear. You can insist on a section in the IEP specifically for "Specially Designed Instruction for the Gifted."
