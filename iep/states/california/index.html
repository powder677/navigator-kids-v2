California IEP Laws: Timelines, Recording & Parent Rights

Short answer: California IEPs operate on strict timelines: 15 days to offer an assessment plan and 60 days to complete it. Parents have the explicit right to record meetings (with 24-hour notice) and can request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) if they disagree with the district's findings.

In plain English: California is a "Timeline State." If the district misses the 60-day deadline to hold your meeting, they may owe you "Compensatory Education" (make-up services). Track every date.

How long does an IEP assessment take in CA?

Short answer: It follows the "15-60 Rule."

  • 15 Calendar Days: From your written request to when the district must give you an Assessment Plan.
  • 60 Calendar Days: From when you sign the plan to when the IEP meeting must be held. (Note: School holidays longer than 5 days pause this clock).

Can I audio record an IEP meeting?

Short answer: Yes. Under California Education Code 56341.1, you have the absolute right to audio record.

The Catch: You must notify the district in writing at least 24 hours before the meeting. If you forget to notify them, they can refuse to let you record.

What if I disagree with the school's testing?

Short answer: You request an IEE (Independent Educational Evaluation).

In California, if you disagree with a district assessment, you can ask for a private evaluation at public expense. The district has only two choices: pay for it, or file for Due Process to prove their report is perfect. Most districts will pay rather than fight.

Is there a new Dyslexia law in California?

Short answer: Yes. SB 237 mandates universal screening for reading difficulties (including dyslexia) for all students in grades K-2 starting in the 2025-26 school year.

What should CA parents do next?

  1. Send your request: Email the principal asking for a "comprehensive assessment." The 15-day clock starts now.
  2. Check the calendar: Calculate exactly when the 60 days are up (excluding summer and long breaks).
  3. Notify to record: If a meeting is scheduled, send your "Notice of Recording" email 24 hours prior.
