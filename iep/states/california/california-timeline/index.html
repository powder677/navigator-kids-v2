California IEP Timeline: 60 Days, 30 Days, or 15 Days?

In California, Special Education math is often harder than Common Core math.

You hear "60 days" from one advocate. You hear "15 days" from a teacher. Meanwhile, you are just waiting for the phone to ring so your child can get help. California Education Code (specifically EC 56043) sets very strict timelines, but unique "Stop-the-Clock" rules can turn a standard evaluation into a 4-month wait if you aren't careful.

Clock 1: The Referral (15 Calendar Days)

The Trigger: You send a written request (email or letter) for a special education assessment. This is a critical step in Advocacy.

The Deadline: The District has 15 calendar days to provide you with a proposed Assessment Plan or a legal refusal (Prior Written Notice).

The "End of Year" Exception

If you send your referral 10 days or fewer before the end of the school year, the clock stops. They don't have to respond until the first 10 days of the next school year. Strategy Tip: Send your requests in August to avoid the summer void.

Clock 2: The Evaluation (60 Calendar Days)

The Trigger: The district receives the Assessment Plan signed by YOU. The clock starts the moment they get it back.

The Deadline: The District has 60 calendar days to assess your child, write reports, and hold the initial State Laws mandated meeting.

The "Stop-the-Clock" Curveball

In California, the timeline pauses for any school break longer than 5 school days. Winter, Spring, and Summer breaks all stop the countdown.

Example: Signing in mid-November often pushes your meeting into February due to the Winter Break pause.

Clock 3: The "Parent Request" Meeting (30 Days)

The Trigger: Your child already has an IEP, but you need to discuss an issue or update the plan.

The Deadline: The District has 30 calendar days to hold that meeting after your written request.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the 60-day timeline include weekends?

Yes, it uses calendar days, but remember the "5-day break" pause rule which is unique to California's implementation of State Laws.

What if the school says they are "short-staffed"?

Under EC 56043, staffing shortages are not a valid excuse for missing a legal deadline. If they pass the date, they are out of compliance.
