Jurisdiction: New Jersey

New Jersey: Where "PRISE" is Your Bible

In the Garden State, your rights are codified in N.J.A.C. 6A:14 and summarized in the PRISE booklet. NJ offers some of the strongest "preview" rights in the country—if you know how to use them.

⏱️ The "20-90-15" Rule: NJ Timelines

New Jersey operates on strict calendar day deadlines (not school days), which keeps the process moving even during breaks.

Action NJ Deadline Parent Strategy
You Request an Evaluation 20 Calendar Days to hold a meeting. The district must hold an "Identification Meeting" (not just respond) within 20 days to decide if evaluations are warranted for your child.
The "Total" Timeline 90 Calendar Days. From the day you sign consent to the day the IEP is implemented, the district has 90 days. This includes testing, eligibility, AND the IEP meeting.
Reviewing Reports 10 Calendar Days PRIOR to meeting. CRITICAL: You have the legal right to receive copies of all evaluation reports 10 days before the eligibility meeting. Do not waive this right!

Your NJ-Specific Powers

📄 The "10-Day Preview" Rule

The Law: N.J.A.C. 6A:14-3.5(a) mandates that schools send you the reports 10 days before the meeting.

The Strategy: If they say "We'll go over the scores at the meeting," cite this code. You need time to read the psych report, Google the terms, and prepare your questions before you walk in the room.

⏳ The "15-Day" Implementation Window

The Law: Once you receive the final IEP, it will automatically go into effect in 15 days unless you file for Mediation or Due Process.

The Strategy: You don't always "sign" the IEP in NJ to make it active. If you disagree, you must file a petition to stop it. Silence is often interpreted as consent.

🎤 Recording the Meeting

The Law: NJ is a "one-party consent" state for wiretapping, but administrative code allows schools to set policies.

The Rule: Case law generally permits parents to record if they give notice. Always email the Case Manager: "I will be recording the meeting to ensure accurate notes."

2e in the Garden State

New Jersey recently strengthened its laws regarding Gifted & Talented education, explicitly protecting 2e students.

The "Strengthening Gifted and Talented Education Act"

The Law: Passed in 2020, this law (N.J.S.A. 18A:35-34) mandates that districts must identify and serve gifted students.

The 2e Leverage: The law specifically states that the identification process must include "students with Individualized Education Plans (IEPs)."

Your Argument: If the school says, "He/She can't be in the G&T program because he/she has a behavioral IEP," they are violating State Statute 18A:35. Quote the Act directly.
