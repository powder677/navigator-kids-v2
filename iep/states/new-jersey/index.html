New Jersey: Where "PRISE" is Your Bible

In the Garden State, your rights are codified in N.J.A.C. 6A:14 and summarized in the PRISE (Parental Rights in Special Education) booklet. New Jersey offers some of the strongest "preview" rights in the country—if you know how to use them.

⏱️ The "20-90-15" Rule: NJ Timelines

New Jersey operates on strict calendar day deadlines. This means the clock keeps ticking during the summer, though it does exclude school holidays for certain response windows.

Your NJ-Specific Powers

📄 The "10-Day Preview" Rule

The Law: N.J.A.C. 6A:14-3.5(a) mandates that schools send you evaluation reports 10 days before the meeting. Note: For annual reviews, they must send a summary of "items to be discussed" 2 days prior.

The Strategy: If they say "we'll explain the scores at the meeting," cite this State Law. You need time to digest the psych report and prepare your questions before walking in the room.

⏳ The "15-Day" Implementation Window

The Law: Once the final IEP is provided, it automatically goes into effect in 15 days unless you file for Mediation or Due Process. In New Jersey, you don't always "sign" the IEP for it to be active.

The Strategy: If you disagree, silence is consent. You must act within that 15-day window to stop the plan. This is where your Battle Plan becomes essential.

2e in the Garden State

New Jersey explicitly protects twice-exceptional students through the Strengthening Gifted and Talented Education Act (N.J.S.A. 18A:35-34).

The G&T Leverage

The law mandates that districts identify gifted students using multiple measures and specifically states this process must include students with IEPs. They cannot use behavioral struggles as a reason to bar your child from gifted programming if they meet the cognitive criteria.
