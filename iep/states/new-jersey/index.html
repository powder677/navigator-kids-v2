New Jersey IEP Laws: PRISE, Timelines & The 10-Day Rule

Short answer: New Jersey special education is governed by N.J.A.C. 6A:14. Key rights include the PRISE booklet (your procedural safeguards), a strict 90-calendar-day timeline for evaluations, and the right to see all reports 10 days before any eligibility meeting.

In plain English: NJ moves fast. Unlike many states that use "school days," NJ uses calendar days. This means the clock keeps ticking on weekends and holidays, forcing schools to complete evaluations quicker.

How long does an IEP evaluation take in NJ?

Short answer: The district has 90 calendar days from the date you sign consent to complete testing, determine eligibility, and implement the IEP.

📅 The "20-Day" Response Rule

When you send a written request for evaluation, the school has 20 calendar days to hold a meeting (Identification Meeting) to decide if they will evaluate. They cannot just ignore your email.

Can I see the reports before the meeting?

Short answer: Yes. Under N.J.A.C. 6A:14-3.5(a), you have the right to receive copies of all evaluation reports and other documentation 10 days prior to the eligibility meeting.

Strategy: If the Case Manager says, "We'll review them together at the meeting," reply via email: "I am invoking my right under NJ code to review the reports 10 days in advance. Please reschedule if they are not ready."

Can I record an IEP meeting in NJ?

Short answer: Yes, generally. New Jersey is a "one-party consent" state. However, schools can set "reasonable rules." Best practice is to notify the Case Manager 24 hours in advance. If you have a recording device visible, they cannot legally stop you.

Can a child with an IEP be in Gifted & Talented?

Short answer: Yes. The Strengthening Gifted and Talented Education Act (N.J.S.A. 18A:35-34) specifically mandates that districts must include students with IEPs in their identification process.

If a school claims a child cannot be in G&T because of behavioral issues or an IEP, they are violating state statute.

What should NJ parents do next?

  1. Download your PRISE: This booklet is your shield. Keep a physical copy in your binder.
  2. Count your days: Use a calendar to mark the 20-day (meeting) and 90-day (IEP) deadlines the moment you hit send on your email.
  3. Email for reports: Send a polite email 15 days before your meeting reminding the team of the 10-day preview rule.
