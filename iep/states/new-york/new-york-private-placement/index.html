Private School Placement in NY: How to Get the District to Pay

In New York City, there is a harsh reality: public schools often lack the specialized programs required for complex needs. With tuition at top-tier NYC private schools now exceeding **$85,000 a year**, most families are priced out.

However, under landmark precedents like Burlington and Carter, you have the right to force the NYC Department of Education (DOE) to pay—if you follow the legal roadmap exactly.

The "Three-Prong Test": How to Win

To win a lawsuit for tuition, you must prove three elements to an Impartial Hearing Officer (IHO):

  • Prong 1: The District Failed. You must prove the DOE's offer (the IEP) was inappropriate and failed to provide FAPE.
  • Prong 2: The Private School Works. You must prove your chosen school provides instruction specifically designed to meet your child's needs. (Note: In NY, the school does not have to be "State Approved").
  • Prong 3: The Equities. You must prove you cooperated in good faith—attending meetings, allowing evaluations, and providing timely notice.

Carter vs. Connors: Which Path Are You On?

How the tuition is paid depends on your financial status and the school's willingness to wait.

Feature Carter Case Connors Case
Payment Parent pays upfront DOE pays school directly
Requirement Financial resources to float tuition Proof of financial hardship
Timeline Reimbursement after victory Prospective funding

The Dealbreaker: The "10-Day Notice" Letter

Under the IDEA, you MUST provide written notice to the DOE at least **10 business days** before you remove your child from public school. Forgetting this step is the most common reason reimbursement is denied.

⚠️ The "Fatal Flaw" Warning

If you sign an enrollment contract before sending your notice or before the 10-day window expires, the district can argue you "predetermined" the placement and refuse to pay. Send the letter, wait the window, then sign.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the private school have to be "State Approved"?

No. In a Carter Case, the school does not need to be on the "Section 853" approved list. It simply must be appropriate for your child's needs. This opens up options for many high-quality specialized schools in the city.

What is a "Nickerson Letter"?

A Nickerson (P-1) letter is a specialized placement authorization issued when the DOE fails to find a seat in a public class within 65 days. Unlike a Carter Case, a Nickerson letter only applies to State-Approved schools.
