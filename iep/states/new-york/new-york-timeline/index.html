New York IEP Timeline: The 60-Day Rule

In New York, delays are not just annoying—they are illegal. While federal law uses loose terms like "reasonable timeframe," New York State Regulations (Part 200.4) and the NYC Jose P. Consent Decree set hard deadlines. If the DOE misses them, you have the power to demand a remedy.

The Clock: 60 Calendar Days

The clock starts the moment the district receives your signed Consent for Evaluation. From that date, the district has 60 calendar days to complete assessments and hold the Eligibility Meeting.

The "Jose P." Power

If you live in NYC, you are protected by Jose P. v. Ambach. This court decree monitors DOE timeliness. If they miss the 60-day window, they are technically in contempt of a court-mandated process. You can use this as leverage in your Battle Plan to secure Related Service Authorizations (RSAs).

Common Delay Tactics

Schools often try to "pause" the clock. Here is the legal reality for 2026:

  • Shortage of Evaluators: Shortages do not excuse the deadline. If they can't find a provider, they must fund an independent evaluator or issue an RSA.
  • Waiting for RtI: Response to Intervention (RtI) cannot be used to delay an evaluation. Once you sign consent, the clock runs regardless of current "building-level" support.
  • Medical Scripts: Lack of a doctor's script for OT or PT should not delay the IEP eligibility meeting itself.

What Happens When Day 61 Hits?

If the deadline passes with no meeting, the district has denied your child a FAPE. In New York, filing for an Impartial Hearing is the standard "fast-forward" button to unblock the bureaucracy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the 60-day timeline include summer?

Generally, the clock stops in July and August unless your child is 12-month (ESY) eligible. However, if consent is signed in May, the DOE still has a responsibility to minimize gaps in Services.

Can I get a private evaluation paid for if they are late?

Yes. If the DOE misses the 60-day deadline, you have a strong case to demand an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at public expense to get the data the school failed to provide. This is a core strategy in our Advocacy modules.
