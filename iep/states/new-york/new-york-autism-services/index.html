Autism Services in NYC: The 12-Month IEP and Extended School Year

June is coming. For most NYC parents, that means camp forms and beach trips. For parents of children with autism, it means the "Summer Slide" anxiety.

In the NYC Department of Education (DOE), getting a 12-Month IEP (Extended School Year or ESY) is not automatic—even with an Autism classification. The system is designed to gatekeep unless you can prove Substantial Regression.

The Standard: It's Not About "Benefit"

The law does not care if your child would "benefit" from summer school. To qualify in New York, you must meet the legal standard defined in Part 200.6(k) of the Commissioner's Regulations:

The "Regression" Definition

A student is eligible only if they would lose so much skill during the summer that it would take at least 8 weeks (the "inordinate period of review") just to get back to where they were in June.

The Strategy: "September Data" is Everything

Most parents lose the fight for summer services in their School Meetings because they lack data. You cannot win on feelings; you win on the recoupment timeline.

The Winning Evidence Chain:

  1. The June Baseline: Progress reports from last June (e.g., "Student masters 10 math facts").
  2. The September Re-entry: First data points from September (e.g., "Student only recalls 2 math facts").
  3. The Recoupment Point: When did they hit 10 facts again? If it's November, you have proven "Substantial Regression."

Don't Wait for the Summer Slide

Frequently Asked Questions

What if my child is in ASD Nest or Horizon?

Nest and Horizon are 10-month programs. ESY is an "add-on" service. Even in these specialized settings, you must prove regression to secure 12-month funding under State Laws.

Can I get summer services just for social skills?

Yes. If your child loses self-regulation or social communication skills over the summer, that counts. Social-emotional regression is just as valid as academic regression in NYC.
