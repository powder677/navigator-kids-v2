Jurisdiction: New York

New York: The "Field Memo" State

In NY, policy is often driven by memos. For 2e parents, one specific memo from 2015 changes everything.

📄 The 2015 "Smoking Gun" Memo

The Document: NYSED Field Advisory: "Students with Disabilities with High Cognition" (June 2015).

What It Says: This memo explicitly forbids districts from denying eligibility based on high cognitive scores. It states that a student can have a "Specific Learning Disability" even with a high IQ.

The Strategy: Print this. Highlight Paragraph 2. When the CSE says, "He/She is too smart for Special Ed," slide this across the table.

The NYC "Turning 5" Process

If you are in NYC, moving from Preschool Special Ed (CPSE) to School-Age (CSE) is known as "Turning 5."

The "Carter Case" Option

If the NYC DOE cannot offer an appropriate placement, you may be eligible for a "Carter Case"—where the city pays for a private Non-Public School (NPS) that specializes in 2e/LD students.

The "Deferral"

For children born late in the year, you may be able to "defer" the move to CSE for one year, keeping your child in their supportive preschool setting. This is critical for 2e kids who need more social maturation.

Don't Waive the "Parent Member"

New York law requires a "Parent Member" (another parent of a child with a disability) to be present at your CSE meeting.

The Trap: They will ask you to waive this. Do not waive it. The Parent Member is your witness and your ally.
