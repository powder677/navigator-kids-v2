New York IEP Timeline: The 60-Day Rule

In New York, delays are not just annoying—they are illegal. While federal law uses loose terms like "reasonable timeframe," New York State Regulations (Part 200.4) and the NYC Jose P. Consent Decree set hard deadlines. If the DOE misses them, you have the power to demand a remedy.

The Clock: 60 Calendar Days

The clock starts the moment the district receives your signed Consent for Evaluation. From that date, the district has 60 calendar days to complete assessments and hold the Eligibility Meeting.

The "Jose P." Power

If you live in NYC, you are protected by Jose P. v. Ambach. If they miss the 60-day window, they are technically in violation of a court-mandated process. You can use this as leverage in your Battle Plan.

Common NYC Delay Tactics

Schools often try to "pause" the clock. Here is the legal reality for 2026:

  • Shortage of Evaluators: Shortages do not excuse the deadline. They must fund an independent evaluator or issue an RSA.
  • Waiting for RtI: Response to Intervention cannot be used to delay an evaluation. Once you sign consent, the clock runs.
