New York IEP Laws: Turning 5, Parent Members & The "High Cognition" Memo

Short answer: New York special education is governed by Part 200 Regulations. Parents have unique rights here, including the "Additional Parent Member" mandate (which schools often try to waive), the critical "Turning 5" transition from preschool, and strong protections for private school tuition reimbursement (Carter Cases).

In plain English: The NYC DOE runs on paperwork and deadlines. If you don't know your specific rights—like refusing to waive the Parent Member—the bureaucracy will steamroll you.

Should I waive the "Additional Parent Member"?

Short answer: No. New York Law guarantees the presence of an "Additional Parent Member"—a trained parent of a child with a disability—at your CSE meeting.

Schools often send a waiver form saying, "This helps us schedule faster." Do not sign it. The Parent Member is the only other person in the room who understands the parent perspective. Waiving them removes your only impartial ally.

Can my smart child get an IEP in NY? (The 2015 Memo)

Short answer: Yes. The 2015 OSEP Memorandum (Letter to Delisle) clarifies that students with high cognition (giftedness) cannot be denied eligibility based solely on passing grades.

If the CSE says, "He's getting good grades, so he doesn't qualify," they are violating federal guidance. You must pivot to "functional performance"—social skills, emotional regulation, and executive function.

What is the "Turning 5" Process?

Short answer: This is the transition from Preschool Special Education (CPSE) to School-Age (CSE) for kindergarten. It is the most dangerous point for service reduction.

The DOE often assumes children have "aged out" of behaviors. You must treat Turning 5 like a brand new initial evaluation. Do not rely on old preschool data; demand new evaluations that reflect current struggles.

What is the IEP timeline in New York?

Short answer: The district has 60 school days (not calendar days) from the date they receive your signed consent to evaluate and implement the IEP. This excludes weekends, holidays, and summer vacation.

What should NYC parents do next?

  1. Identify your CSE Email: Find your district office email (not just the school principal) to send the request.
  2. Prepare for the Social History: This is the first interview. Frame your child's struggles clearly—do not sugarcoat "for the record."
  3. Download the "Procedural Safeguards": Read the section on Due Process so you know your leverage before you walk into the meeting.
