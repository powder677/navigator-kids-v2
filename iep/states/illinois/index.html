Jurisdiction: Illinois

Illinois: The Land of Lincoln (and "Domain Meetings")

In Illinois, the battle isn't just the IEP meeting—it's the meeting before the meeting. Understanding the ISBE (Illinois State Board of Education) rules on "Domains" is the difference between a full evaluation and a partial one for your child.

⏱️ The "14-60" Rule: Illinois Timelines

Illinois uses School Days (days students are in attendance), which makes the process longer than you might think.

Action Illinois Deadline Parent Strategy
You Request an Evaluation 14 School Days to respond. The district must decide whether to evaluate and provide you with a written response (and usually schedule the Domain Meeting).
The Evaluation Process 60 School Days to complete. The clock starts when you sign the Consent for Evaluation. Note: If there are fewer than 60 days left in the school year, the report is due by the start of the next year.
IEP Implementation 10 School Days notice. Once an IEP is developed, the school must give you 10 days notice before implementation (unless you waive this wait time).

Your Illinois-Specific Powers

⚠️ The "Domain Meeting" Trap

The Event: Before testing begins, you will attend a "Domain Review" meeting to decide what to test.

The Trap: They list 8 domains (Academic, Functional, Hearing, Vision, Social/Emotional, etc.). If the "Social/Emotional" box is NOT checked, they will not evaluate your child's anxiety or behavior.

The Strategy: Do not let them skip domains. If you suspect Autism or ADHD, you must demand the "Social/Emotional" and "Functional Performance" domains be checked.

🎤 Recording: The "Two-Party" Warning

The Law: Illinois is a strict "Two-Party Consent" state for audio recording. You cannot record an IEP meeting secretly. It is a crime.

The Strategy: Most districts have policies prohibiting recording unless it is a necessary accommodation for a parent's disability. Check your specific district's Board Policy (usually Section 7).

👩‍⚖️ IEE (Independent Evaluation)

The Law: If you disagree with the school's evaluation, you can request an IEE at public expense.

The IL Twist: Illinois districts are aggressive about defending their evaluations. If you ask for an IEE, they often file for Due Process against you rather than just paying for it. Be prepared.

2e in Illinois

Illinois has recently strengthened laws for high-achieving students, which is a massive win for 2e advocacy.

The "Accelerated Placement Act"

The Law: The Accelerated Placement Act (Public Act 100-0421) requires districts to have a policy for identifying and serving high-ability students.

The 2e Leverage: Crucially, the law states that participation cannot be conditioned on a disability. This means they cannot say, "He/She can't be in Advanced Math because he/she has an IEP for behavior." If he/she meets the math standards, he/she gets the math placement. Period.

Automatic Enrollment: Recent amendments now require "automatic enrollment" in advanced courses if a student meets state standards on standardized tests.
