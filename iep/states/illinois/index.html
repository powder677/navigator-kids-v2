Illinois: The Land of Lincoln (and "Domain Meetings")

In Illinois, the battle isn't just the IEP meeting—it's the meeting before the meeting. Understanding the ISBE (Illinois State Board of Education) rules on "Domains" is the difference between a full evaluation and a partial one for your child.

⏱️ The "14-60" Rule: Illinois Timelines

Illinois uses School Days (days students are in attendance), which makes the process longer than calendar-day states. Here is the schedule you must hold the school to:

Action Illinois Deadline Parent Strategy
You Request Evaluation 14 School Days The district must decide to evaluate and usually schedules the Domain Meeting within this window.
The Evaluation Clock 60 School Days Clock starts when you sign Consent. If < 60 days remain in the year, it is due by start of next year.
IEP Implementation 10 School Days Notice period before services start. You can waive this to start IEP services immediately.

⚠️ The "Domain Meeting" Trap

The Event: Before testing begins, you will attend a "Domain Review." This decides what they test. Illinois looks at 8 domains including Academic, Functional, Social/Emotional, and Cognitive.

The Trap: If the "Social/Emotional" or "Functional Performance" boxes aren't checked, they won't evaluate for ADHD or Autism traits. Strategy: Use your Battle Plan to demand every area of suspected disability be checked before signing.

🎤 Recording: The "Two-Party" Warning

The Law: Illinois is a "Two-Party Consent" state. You cannot record an IEP meeting secretly—it is a crime. Recording is usually only allowed as a disability accommodation for the parent.

The Strategy: Instead of recording, bring a second person to take notes or request a written meeting summary be attached to the State Laws mandated forms.

2e in Illinois: The Accelerated Placement Act

Illinois has a powerful tool for twice-exceptional students: Public Act 100-0421. This law requires districts to allow high-ability students access to advanced coursework, regardless of their IEP status.

Automatic Enrollment Rights

As of the 2023-24 school year, students who exceed state standards on assessments must be automatically enrolled in the next most rigorous level of advanced coursework. They cannot bar your child from advanced math just because they have a behavioral 504 or IEP.
