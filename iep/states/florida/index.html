Florida: Where "Gifted" is Special Education

In the Sunshine State, we use the term ESE (Exceptional Student Education). Unlike most states, Florida law classifies "Gifted" under the ESE umbrella, granting gifted students many of the same procedural safeguards as students with disabilities.

⏱️ The "30-60" Rule: Florida Timelines

Florida strict statutes govern how long a school can make you wait (Rule 6A-6.0331).

Your Florida-Specific Powers

🧠 The "Gifted is ESE" Advantage

The Law: In Florida, Gifted is an ESE program. This means 2e kids are technically "Double ESE."

The Strategy: If the school tries to separate your child into an EP (Educational Plan) for giftedness and a 504 for the disability, push back. You can demand a comprehensive IEP that covers both cognitive needs and disability support in one legal document.

💸 The "Nuclear Option": FES-UA

The Program: The Family Empowerment Scholarship for Students with Unique Abilities.

The Power: If the public school cannot meet your child's needs, Florida allows you to take state funding (often $10k+) for private school or therapy. The mere existence of this option often makes districts find the "budget" for an aide they previously denied.

📊 The Matrix of Services

Every IEP student in Florida gets a "Matrix Score" which determines the funding the school receives. Strategy: Ask to see the Matrix score. If your child is scored as high-needs (254 or 255) but you are being told there's "no budget" for 1:1 support, use that data to prove they are receiving funds they aren't spending on your child.

2e in the Sunshine State

Florida's "Plan B" for Gifted eligibility is a massive tool for twice-exceptional families whose kids' IQ scores might be suppressed by their disability.

Plan B: The Gifted Loophole

Most qualify for Gifted with an IQ of 130. But Plan B (Rule 6A-6.03019) allows a matrix of criteria (creativity, teacher checklists) to replace strict IQ cutoffs if a disability is masking potential.
