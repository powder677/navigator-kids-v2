Florida IEP Laws (ESE): Plan B Gifted & Timelines

Short answer: Florida refers to special education as ESE (Exceptional Student Education). Unique to Florida, "Gifted" is an ESE category. Key rights include the 60-day evaluation timeline, the "Plan B" pathway for 2e gifted students, and the Matrix of Services which determines school funding.

In plain English: Florida is one of the few states where being "Gifted" gives you almost the same legal rights as having a disability. If you have a 2e child, you have double the leverage.

How long does an IEP evaluation take in Florida?

Short answer: The district has 60 calendar days from the date they receive your signed consent to complete the evaluation.

📅 The "30-Day" Response Rule

When you request an evaluation, the school has 30 calendar days to either get your consent to evaluate OR give you a written refusal explaining why. They cannot just stall.

What is "Plan B" for Gifted Eligibility?

Short answer: "Plan A" requires an IQ of 130. "Plan B" (Rule 6A-6.03019) is for students from underrepresented groups or those with disabilities (2e) whose test scores may be depressed.

Critical Strategy: If your child has ADHD/Autism and scores a 125, do not accept a denial. Demand eligibility under Plan B, which uses a "matrix" of criteria (creativity checklists, grades) instead of a hard IQ cutoff.

What is the "Matrix of Services"?

Short answer: This is a form (unique to FL) that scores the intensity of services your child needs (251-255). It determines how much state funding the school gets for your child.

Strategy: Always ask for a copy of the Matrix. If the school says they "can't afford" an aide, check the Matrix score. If your child is a 254 or 255, the school is receiving thousands in extra funding specifically for that level of support.

What if the public school fails my child? (FES-UA)

Short answer: You can leave. The FES-UA (Family Empowerment Scholarship for Students with Unique Abilities) allows you to take state funding (often $10k+) to pay for private school, therapy, or tutoring.

Mentioning that you are "exploring your FES-UA options" often motivates a reluctant IEP team to offer better services to keep your student (and the funding) in the district.

What should FL parents do next?

  1. Request Plan B: If your 2e child was denied gifted, email the ESE director specifically asking for a "Plan B Eligibility Review."
  2. Check the Calendar: FL pauses the 60-day clock for breaks of 5+ days (like Thanksgiving or Spring Break). Adjust your count accordingly.
  3. Apply for FES-UA: Even if you stay public, apply for the scholarship to establish your "Plan B" (exit strategy).
