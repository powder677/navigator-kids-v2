Texas ARD Laws: What Parents Need to Know

Short answer: Texas calls IEP meetings "ARD" meetings. Parents have unique rights here, including the right to record meetings (one-party consent), the "10-Day Recess" rule for disagreements, and mandatory curriculum modifications for gifted students under Section 4.9A.

In plain English: Texas gives you a "Pause Button." If you don't like what the school proposes, you can legally force a 10-day break to gather more evidence. You do not have to sign the IEP that day.

What is the difference between ARD and IEP?

Short answer: Nothing but the name. Texas calls the meeting the Admission, Review, and Dismissal (ARD) committee. The document they produce is still an IEP. Federal IDEA law applies to every ARD meeting just as it does to an IEP meeting.

What if I disagree with the decision? (The 10-Day Rule)

Short answer: You invoke the 10-Day Recess. Under Texas Administrative Code §89.1050(g), if families and schools disagree, the parent can demand a recess of up to 10 school days. The committee must reconvene to try to reach a consensus.

Advocate Tip: Do not check "Disagree" and sign on the spot. Say: "We are not in agreement. I am invoking the 10-day recess rule to allow both sides to gather more data."

Must Texas schools modify curriculum for gifted students?

Short answer: Yes. Section 4.9A of the Texas State Plan for the Education of Gifted/Talented Students mandates that curriculum must be modified for depth and complexity.

If the school says, "We don't modify for gifted kids," they are violating state policy. This is the strongest lever for twice-exceptional (2e) parents in Texas.

Can I record an ARD meeting in Texas?

Short answer: Yes. Texas is a "one-party consent" state (Penal Code §16.02), so you do not legally need permission. However, many school boards have local policies banning "secret" recordings.

Best practice: Notify them 24 hours in advance via email: "I will be recording our upcoming ARD meeting to ensure I have accurate records for my child's education."

Should my child have a 504 or IEP for Dyslexia?

Short answer: Following recent legal updates (HB 3928), Texas schools must refer students suspected of dyslexia for a full special education evaluation (IEP), not just a 504.

A 504 Plan provides access (audiobooks). An IEP provides instruction (a therapist teaching them to read). If your child cannot read on grade level, they need an IEP.

Common Questions Texas Parents Ask

Who attends an ARD meeting?

By law, the committee must include: the parent, a regular ed teacher, a special ed teacher, a district representative (admin), and someone who can interpret evaluation results (diag/LSSP).

How much notice must I get before a meeting?

Texas schools must provide at least 5 school days written notice before an ARD meeting, stating the time, place, and purpose.
