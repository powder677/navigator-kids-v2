Jurisdiction: Texas

Texas: The "State Plan" is Your Weapon

Most advocates look at IDEA. In Texas, 2e parents must look at the Texas State Plan for the Education of Gifted/Talented Students.

📜 The "Hidden" Mandate: Section 4.9A

The Source: Texas State Plan for the Education of Gifted/Talented Students

The Mandate: Section 4.9A explicitly requires that curriculum must be modified for gifted students.

The Strategy: If the ARD committee says, "We can't modify the curriculum for him/her just because he/she is bored," you pull out Section 4.9A. It is not a favor; it is a state requirement.

🎤 Recording: The "Legal but Banned" Gray Area

State Law: One-Party Consent

Under Texas Penal Code § 16.02, Texas is a "One-Party Consent" state. Legally, you do not need the other person's permission to record a conversation you are part of.

Local Policy: The Trap

However, many school boards (like HISD or DISD) have local policies prohibiting recording. While they likely cannot prosecute you (due to state law), they can end the meeting if you are caught.

Advice: Notify them. "I am recording this ARD for my own records." Most will simply record you back.

The "Dyslexia 504" Pipeline

Texas schools historically push Dyslexic/ADHD students into Section 504 plans to avoid Special Ed numbers.

Your Counter: A 504 offers access (audiobooks). An IEP offers instruction (reading therapy). If your child needs to be taught how to read, a 504 is insufficient. Demand an IEP based on "Educational Need."
